# Retooling AquaWatch for Jamaica's Seventy-Percent Water Loss

AquaWatch becomes useful to the National Water Commission (NWC) when it stops being a point-wise anomaly classifier on hand-made data and becomes a **DMA-level night-flow and residual monitor**. That monitor would be tested on physically simulated and public benchmark leaks, scored by event recall against a fixed false-alarm budget, built with reproducible MLOps, and fed only by read-only, aggregate telemetry under a no-cost pilot agreement. The stakes are large. NWC officials put islandwide non-revenue water (NRW) at **70-73%** in October 2026. The government's Water Compact targets **at most 65% by 2030 and 30% by 2040**. Only the Miya co-managed zones of Kingston & St Andrew (KSA) and Portmore have been brought down to about **38-40%**. An 11-year islandwide performance-based NRW programme is in procurement, with audits and first works expected in 2027/28, and its contractors and NWC's monitoring unit are AquaWatch's realistic users. The current prototype has five structural gaps. It ignores the minimum-night-flow method the industry actually uses. It treats a persistent leak as a set of independent hourly outliers. It uses ground-truth labels to filter its training data. It reports metrics that cannot support any claim with six events. And it ships a pickled model with no CI. None of these is hard to fix. The roadmap at the end orders the fixes so a six-person student team can make AquaWatch credible within one semester and pilot-ready within two.

## Seventy percent of NWC's water earns nothing, and DMAs are where it is recovered

NWC defines NRW as water "produced, treated and pumped but never reaches a paying customer" and reports it mainly as a percentage of production ([JIS, Oct 2026](https://jis.gov.jm/nwc-working-to-reduce-water-loss/)). The figure has not moved in years. The OUR's 2023-24 report put it at **74.3%** with no improvement over six years, and the January 2024 islandwide tender cited **80%** ([Gleaner, Jan 2024](https://past.jamaica-gleaner.com/article/business/20240112/water-utility-targets-resorts-and-schemes-decade-long-revenue-improvement)). NWC told Parliament's Public Accounts Committee 70-73% in October 2026 ([Jamaica Observer, Oct 2026](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2026/10/21/nwc-seeks-51-hike/)). The Water Compact calls the level "unacceptably high" and commits to performance-based contracts that "link funds to actual gains on the ground" ([Water Compact 2026](https://www.worldbank.org/content/dam/theworldbankgroupasset/document/2026/water-forward-compacts/Water%20Compact%20Jamaica_2026.pdf)). The scale is about **11,000 km of pipe** and more than 1,000 treatment and pumping facilities ([Gleaner editorial, Jun 2026](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/index%2Ephp/article/commentary/20260612/editorial-nwc-audit-reveals-lie)). This is happening while NWC asks the OUR for a **51% tariff increase over five years**, with a J$6.3B operating loss and negative equity ([Jamaica Observer, 25 Oct 2026](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2026/10/25/facing-heat-rate-request-nwc-unveils-5-pillar-plan/)). Credible, auditable NRW evidence is therefore politically salient right now, but NWC has little money for expensive platforms.

What works in Jamaica is already documented. The US$42.5M KSA contract (2015) and the US$13M Portmore contract (2021-2026) with Miya used district metered areas (DMAs), pressure zones managed with a hydraulic model, NWC crews doing active leak detection with Miya equipment, and meter replacement. Portmore set up **12 pressure zones and 30 DMAs**, cut the leak backlog **from 3,000 to 150**, and reduced system demand from about 42,000 to 32,000 m³/day for the same population ([JIS, Jan 2023](https://jis.gov.jm/features/nwc-miya-non-revenue-water-reduction-programme-yielding-positive-outcomes/)). KSA losses fell from above 120,000 m³/day to under 70,000 m³/day, and by 2020 NWC was saving about **US$8M a year** ([Gleaner via PressReader, Jun 2020](https://www.pressreader.com/jamaica/jamaica-gleaner/20200603/281505048440807)). The upcoming islandwide programme will measure success by NRW level per system and by **the number of customers with continuous supply** ([Gleaner, Jan 2024](https://past.jamaica-gleaner.com/article/business/20240112/water-utility-targets-resorts-and-schemes-decade-long-revenue-improvement)). AquaWatch should therefore fit into the active leakage control loop: flag a DMA whose night flow is rising, send a crew to survey it, repair, and verify the drop. It should not position itself as a standalone "AI leak detector".

The industry measures this with the IWA water balance. Water losses split into apparent losses (theft, meter error) and real losses (leaks and bursts). Real losses are benchmarked with the Infrastructure Leakage Index, ILI = CARL/UARL, where **UARL = (18·Lm + 0.8·Nc + 25·Lp)·P** litres/day ([Autodesk Info360 docs](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/INF360I-UserGuide/files/GUID-9EAF65E8-2C26-45AC-863B-9A160EA21EEA.htm)). The World Bank Institute treats ILI ≥16 as high priority in developing countries ([World Bank NRW deck](https://ppp.worldbank.org/public-private-partnership/sites/ppp.worldbank.org/files/2022-03/1_Basics_in_NRW___Water_Balance.pdf)). The tool's daily output per DMA should therefore be a KPI pack, not a flag column:

- minimum night flow (MNF) and its trend
- estimated night leakage
- NRW in m³/day and as a percentage (NWC's headline figure)
- litres per connection per day
- ILI where pressure and asset data exist
- supply hours (the programme's continuity KPI)
- leak backlog and volume recovered after repairs

Two Jamaican realities change the technical design. The first is **intermittent supply**. Parts of KSA went on a 12-hours-a-day schedule in 2023 ([Observer, Mar 2023](https://jamaicaobserver.com/2023/03/09/our-demands-answers-from-nwc-on-unscheduled-water-lock-offs)), lock-offs persisted in the 2026 drought ([Observer, Oct 2026](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2026/10/09/taps-run-dry-suffers-20261009-1204-206851/)), and the Compact's reliability standard is only "24 hours per day, or otherwise on a predictable schedule" ([Water Compact 2026](https://www.worldbank.org/content/dam/theworldbankgroupasset/document/2026/water-forward-compacts/Water%20Compact%20Jamaica_2026.pdf)). When a zone is shut at night, MNF is undefined, and the refill surge when supply resumes looks like a burst. The second is **theft and meter problems**. NWC removed more than 86 illegal connections in 2023, with up to 3 million gallons lost on some days ([Gleaner, Apr 2023](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/article/lead-stories/20230426/millions-down-drain-crooks-plunder-nwc)). In Q1 2026 it logged **5,883 Guaranteed Standards breaches**, led by meter reading and estimated bills ([OUR Q1 2026](https://our.org.jm/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-Jan-Mar-Quarterly-Performance-Report_FINAL.pdf)). A sustained flow step in a Jamaican DMA should be labelled "unexplained consumption (leak or illegal connection)", not "leak". The detector also needs supply-schedule masks, plus regime flags for hurricanes and droughts, so that a period like Hurricane Melissa (more than J$10B in damage, [Gleaner, Nov 2025](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20251113/cost-nwc-hurricane-melissa-top-10-billion)) does not end up in the training baseline as "normal".

The biggest unknown is NWC's own data. No public source describes an islandwide SCADA system, an AMI head-end, or logger resolution. NWC is installing solid-state meters "with capacity for remote reading" ([Observer, Nov 2023](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2023/11/11/nwc-continues-to-install-new-water-meters-islandwide/)), and Diehl ran a 525-unit fixed-network pilot at Long Mountain ([Diehl case study](https://www.diehl.com/metering/en/case-studies/jamaica-fixed-network)). The safe assumption is sparse data: DMA inflow at 15-minute or hourly resolution in KSA and Portmore, production and bulk meters elsewhere, monthly billing, and gaps. AquaWatch should be designed to work on that.

## Night flow, residuals and CUSUM beat a memoryless forest on two features

MNF analysis of DMA inflow is the industry baseline. It measures inflow in a low-demand window, usually somewhere in 01:00-05:00 ([Bentley](https://blog.bentley.com/software/mnfs-and-dmas-new-concepts)), compares it with a baseline, and flags step increases. Real leaks can be small relative to the signal. One documented case showed MNF rising from **76.5 to 85.2 L/s, about +11%**, which indicated a new leak ([US patent 10401250](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10401250)). AquaWatch's "flow 25% above the zone/hour mean at any hour" rule would miss leaks of that size, because daytime demand variability swamps the leak flow. The cheapest credible upgrade is a dedicated night-window feature: mean inflow over 02:00-04:00 local time, compared with a rolling 14-28-night median plus k times the median absolute deviation (MAD).

The research literature calls the dominant method "prediction-classification": forecast the expected flow, then run a statistical test on the residual (CUSUM, EWMA, or Western Electric rules). Wu & Liu's review names it the most popular category and identifies label scarcity as the core obstacle ([summary](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/a-review-of-data-driven-approaches-for-burst-detection-in-water-distribution-systems/811065243181514755-3159)). Real-world performance is modest. On Dutch utility data, an adaptive-forecast heuristic caught **44.4% of reported bursts at a 5% false-alarm rate** and beat CUSUM on false alarms ([Bakker et al.](https://pure.korea.ac.kr/en/publications/detecting-pipe-bursts-using-heuristic-and-cusum-methods/)). AquaWatch's deviation-from-zone/hour-mean features are already a weak residual. The improvements are a better forecast (hour-of-week, holidays, a rolling baseline) and a sequential test that accumulates evidence over time.

Isolation Forest is a legitimate choice. UCL applied it to more than 2,500 Yorkshire Water DMAs and checked the results against repair logs ([McMillan et al.](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10174209/1/McMillan_Machine-Learning-Based%20Health%20Monitoring%20and%20Leakage%20Management%20of%20Water%20Distribution%20Systems_VoR.pdf)). Joseph et al. found its outliers correlated with known repairs, but only after removing industrial use and smoothing filter-backwash spikes ([MDPI Water 17(14):2164](https://mdpi.com/2073-4441/17/14/2164)). No published head-to-head shows it beating forecast residuals plus CUSUM. Its weakness for this problem is that it scores each hour independently, while a leak is a persistent level shift. The fix is to give the forest rolling-window features (24-hour mean residual, night residual, slope, the CUSUM statistic itself) and to run CUSUM alongside it as a baseline. Further feature priorities are a joint "flow up and pressure down" indicator, because leak flow depends on pressure ([Autodesk](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/INF360I-UserGuide/files/GUID-9EAF65E8-2C26-45AC-863B-9A160EA21EEA.htm)), and neighbour-zone correlation, so that a shift seen in every zone is read as demand or weather rather than a leak.

The BattLeDIM 2020 competition sets the limit on what to claim. No single method dominated. The strong entries paired statistical detection with localisation using a calibrated hydraulic model, and the organisers concluded that leak detection and isolation methods "need further development" ([Vrachimis et al. 2022](https://zenodo.org/record/6641304)). Without a calibrated EPANET model, AquaWatch localises only to zone level. Hourly data also cannot see pressure transients, which last seconds and need high-rate sensors like those in the MIT/PUB WaterWiSe deployment ([Allen et al. 2013](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/1721.1/92758/1/Allen%20et%20al%20-%20SNPD2013.pdf)). AquaWatch should present itself as the **detection and zone-prioritisation layer** that tells crews where to deploy acoustic equipment or follow up on satellite survey areas.

Commercial products show what "industry standard" output looks like. TaKaDu, Autodesk Info360 and Xylem produce **managed events**, not anomaly flags. Each event has a type (leak, burst, meter fault, boundary valve), a start time, an estimated flow and volume, a zone, a status workflow, and closure KPIs. Info360 computes a daily ILI from a zone mass balance ([Autodesk](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/INF360I-UserGuide/files/GUID-9EAF65E8-2C26-45AC-863B-9A160EA21EEA.htm)). Xylem uses water balance to tell a leak apart from a boundary valve left open ([Xylem](https://www.xylem.com/en-ca/resources/white-papers/optimizing-leak-detection-using-virtual-district-metering-areas-dmas/)). In TaKaDu's Knoxville case, **12 days** passed between the alert and isolating the leak ([KUB case study](https://takadu.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/TaKaDu-and-KUB-case-study-April-2019.pdf)), which shows that the field response loop is part of the product. ASTERRA's satellite programme with Affinity Water screened 9,045 km and found 902 leaks while crews walked under 4% of the network ([SUEZ](https://www.suez.com/en/uk/case-studies/satellite-leak-detection-delivers-results-affinity-water)), and it has been presented at the Caribbean water-loss conference ([CRWLC 2023](https://www.mpu.gov.tt/crwlc2023/files/talks/Using%20Satellite%20RADAR%20Imagery%20to%20Detect%20Leaks%20from%20Potable%20Water%20Pipes%20-%20United%20States%20-%20John%20Stevens.pdf)). Nearly all of these results are vendor-reported, and no vendor publishes precision or recall. AquaWatch's open evaluation can therefore be a real differentiator.

## Simulated hydraulics and BattLeDIM replace a 14-day hand-made CSV

Real telemetry is messier than the prototype assumes. Field sensors report flow in L/s or m³/h and pressure in metres of head or bar. They do not report dimensionless "index" values. A utility meter-data quality study found **about 10% of readings missing and 3-35% dubious**, with flatlines and timestamp errors the most common faults ([Urban Water Journal 2019](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/1573062X.2019.1611884)). Many spikes come from operations, such as filter backwash, rather than faults ([Unibo](https://cris.unibo.it/bitstream/11585/1020130/1/Combining%20SCADA%20and%20smart%20meter%20data%20to%20improve%20anomaly%20detection.pdf)). A realistic schema is long format (`timestamp_utc, zone_id, sensor_id, quantity, value, unit, quality_flag`) stored as Parquet, and it borrows the OGC SensorThings model of Thing, Datastream and Observation ([WMO 2024](https://public.wmo.int/sites/default/files/2024-02/SensorThings%20API%20and%20%20WaterML2.0%20.pdf)). Timestamps should be stored in UTC, with night windows computed in Jamaica local time (UTC-5, no daylight saving). Gaps should be flagged, never forward-filled.

Two open, labelled benchmarks should become standard test sets. **BattLeDIM/L-Town** comes from a real Cypriot network. It provides a year of 5-minute SCADA data for 2018 and another for 2019 from 33 pressure sensors, 3 flow sensors, 1 tank level and 82 AMR meters, together with leak lists, a 2018 repair report and the EPANET model, under **CC BY 4.0 (550 MB)** ([Zenodo 4017658](https://zenodo.org/record/4017658); [rules](https://battledim.ucy.ac.cy/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/BattLeDIM_Problem_Description_and_Rules-v1.3.pdf)). EPyT-Flow's loader reproduces the official leaderboard scoring ([EPyT-Flow](https://epyt-flow.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_modules/epyt_flow/data/benchmarks/battledim.html)). **KIOS LeakDB** provides 1,000 scenarios each on Net1 and Hanoi, with 0/1 labels every 30 minutes (13.9 GB, CC BY 4.0) ([Zenodo 13985057](https://zenodo.org/records/13985057)). It can be downloaded in subsets through WaterBenchmarkHub ([docs](https://waterbenchmarkhub.readthedocs.io/en/dev/water_benchmark_hub.leakdb.html)). Both datasets are simulated. No public dataset contains real, utility-confirmed leaks at network scale ([survey, arXiv 2410.12461](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12461)), and AquaWatch's claims should say so.

For Jamaica-specific scenarios, the hand-made CSV should be replaced by hydraulic simulation in **WNTR** (USEPA/Sandia, BSD licence). Use pressure-dependent demand (`demand_model = 'PDD'`), which is required when supply is cut, because demand-driven mode produces impossible negative pressures. Add leaks with `junction.add_leak(wn, area, start_time, end_time)` in the WNTRSimulator, and run at 300-900-second time steps ([WNTR hydraulics docs](https://usepa.github.io/WNTR/hydraulics.html)). The recipe is:

1. Build a small network with five DMAs, each with a metered inlet and two or three pressure nodes.
2. Use residential demand patterns with weekday and weekend variants and noise for each zone.
3. Schedule supply on/off windows to mimic lock-offs.
4. Add abrupt leaks, plus incipient leaks that grow through pause/restart.
5. Simulate 30-365 days.
6. Post-process a sensor layer with noise, drift, gaps of about 1-10%, flatlines, spikes and timestamp shifts, which are the fault classes the quality study documents.
7. Export ground-truth events to a separate labels table, mirroring BattLeDIM's layout.

The noise magnitudes are engineering assumptions, not sourced values, and the datasheet should say so.

## Event recall at a fixed alarm budget, not point-wise F1

Point-wise precision and recall on hourly rows measure the wrong thing. A leak is one contiguous event, and what matters is whether it is caught and how fast. The usual ML fix, point-adjusted F1, is worse. Kim et al. showed that **uniform random scores reach PA-F1 of 0.80-0.97** on standard benchmarks, against plain F1 of 0.08-0.23, and that untrained models match published state of the art ([Kim et al., AAAI 2022](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2109.05257)). AquaWatch's headline metrics should be:

- **Event recall**: a leak counts as detected if at least one alarm falls within T_max of its start.
- **Alarm precision**: consecutive flagged hours are merged into one episode, and repeat alarms on a leak already caught score zero, as in BattLeDIM.
- **False alarms per DMA per week**, measured on leak-free periods.
- **Time to detection**: median and interquartile range.
- **Volume lost before detection**, in m³.
- **Minimum detectable leak**: the smallest injected leak, as a percentage of mean DMA flow, caught in at least 90% of trials.

Secondary metrics are front-biased range precision/recall ([Tatbul et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1803.03639)), affiliation F1, which resists the gaming that range metrics allow ([Huet et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13167)), and threshold-free VUS-PR ([Paparrizos et al.](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol15/p2774-paparrizos.pdf)). Point-wise F1 can stay for continuity only.

Utilities value false-alarm control above recall, and BattLeDIM's economic scoring encodes this. Each detection earns the water saved from detection time to the leak's end and loses crew cost for every false alarm. Under the official rules, **random alarms once a day score about -€34k** while a perfect alarm set scores about €179k on 2018 data ([EPyT-Flow issue #34](https://github.com/WaterFutures/EPyT-Flow/issues/34)). One competition entry had the highest true-positive rate but **138 false positives** and lost on economics ([LWDA 2023](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3630/LWDA2023-paper40.pdf)). AquaWatch should publish a recall-versus-false-alarms-per-week curve, choose its operating point on validation data, and report a simplified Jamaican economic score: Σ_TP(C_water·V_saved − C_crew) − Σ_FP·C_crew, with stated assumptions about NWC tariffs and crew costs.

The validation design also needs to change. The single 10/4-day split should become **expanding-window walk-forward folds**: train on the past, set the threshold on an inner validation block for a target alarm rate, leave a gap of at least 24 hours, then test. Kim et al. specifically criticise choosing thresholds on the test set ([Kim et al.](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2109.05257)), and tuning Isolation Forest's `contamination` value on test results does exactly that. The current code has a related leak. `run_detection` in `src/model.py` filters training rows with `simulated_leak_label == 0`, which uses ground truth that a real utility would not have. It should be replaced with a label-free robust filter, for example dropping rows whose robust z-score within the same hour-of-week exceeds 3.5, or by keeping all rows. Fourteen days cannot give each hour-of-week slot the three to four weeks of history it needs for stable statistics, and claims about seasonal robustness need at least a year that includes Jamaica's wet and dry seasons.

With six events, uncertainty dominates. By exact binomial arithmetic, three of four leaks detected has a 95% confidence interval of roughly **19-99%**. Event recall should be reported as k/n with Clopper-Pearson or Wilson intervals. Use a block bootstrap over days or weeks, never individual hours, for false-alarm rates. Use McNemar's exact test for paired comparisons of the model and the rule on the same leaks. Hundreds of synthetic injections should be run and reported separately, labelled "simulated". Every detector should be run up a **baseline ladder** with the same inputs and the same protocol:

1. random alarms at a matched rate
2. static threshold
3. MNF rule
4. seasonal-naive residual
5. CUSUM
6. flow-and-pressure rule
7. Isolation Forest on flow only
8. Isolation Forest on flow and pressure

This separates the effect of adding pressure from the effect of using a model, which the current comparison of a flow-only rule against a flow-and-pressure forest mixes together. The results belong in a model card ([Mitchell et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993)), broken down by night/day, leak size, DMA and season, and in a datasheet that states the labels are synthetic ([Gebru et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09010)).

## From a committed joblib to an ML Test Score above zero

Against Google's ML Test Score, which has 28 tests across Data, Model, Infrastructure and Monitoring and scores the **minimum** of the four sections, AquaWatch today scores **0, "more of a research project"** ([Breck et al.](https://storage.googleapis.com/gweb-research2023-media/pubtools/4156.pdf)). It sits at Microsoft MLOps Level 0, whose definition, "a single model file handed off manually", describes the committed `models/isolation_forest.joblib` ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/architecture/ai-ml/guide/mlops-maturity-model)). Because the score is the minimum section, the team should give each section an owner rather than perfecting tests while ignoring monitoring. A realistic target is a score of 1-2 and Microsoft Level 2 (automated training, tracked experiments, versioned models).

The pickle is the first thing to remove. scikit-learn warns that pickle and joblib loading can execute arbitrary code, and that no format loads safely across scikit-learn versions. It recommends skops.io for safer loading ([scikit-learn model persistence](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/model_persistence.html)). OWASP lists tampered ML artefacts and dependencies as supply-chain attacks ([OWASP ML06](https://owasp.org/www-project-machine-learning-security-top-10/docs/ML06_2023-AI_Supply_Chain_Attacks.html)). The model should become a build output saved with skops, with a metadata JSON recording the data hash, git SHA, seed, library versions and metrics. The CSV should move to DVC ([CD4ML](https://martinfowler.com/articles/cd4ml.html)), and runs should be tracked in MLflow with `@champion`/`@challenger` aliases, so that rollback means re-pointing an alias ([MLflow registry](https://mlflow.org/docs/latest/ml/model-registry)).

Packaging moves from the loose `requirements.txt` to `pyproject.toml` with a committed `uv.lock`. Dependabot supports uv for version updates and, since December 2025, for security updates ([GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-12-16-dependabot-security-updates-now-support-uv/)). Dependabot's uv support can rewrite lower bounds in `pyproject.toml`, so CI should run `uv lock --check` ([Classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/en/articles/dependabot-uv-versioning-pitfall/)). A GitHub Actions workflow on every PR should run ruff, a lenient type check, pytest with coverage, pip-audit and the lock check, with branch protection on `main`. That reaches Microsoft Level 1. Testing should follow CD4ML's four categories ([CD4ML](https://martinfowler.com/articles/cd4ml.html)):

- **Data validation**: a pandera schema (current release 0.30.x, [pandera](https://pandera.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)) for units, ranges, monotonic timestamps and null rates, applied at both training and scoring time.
- **Component and UI tests**: Streamlit's native `AppTest` for headless UI smoke tests in CI ([Streamlit docs](https://docs.streamlit.io/develop/concepts/app-testing)), plus a test that training twice with a fixed seed gives identical predictions.
- **Model-quality gates**: event recall on injected leaks and a check that a simpler baseline does not beat the model (ML Test Score "Model 5").

The production shape is micro-batch, not streaming. Data arrives by scheduled pull or SFTP/CSV drop first and MQTT later. It is stored in TimescaleDB or Parquet and scored every 5-15 minutes by a worker behind a FastAPI service. Alerts are deduplicated into incidents with hysteresis and maintenance-window suppression, and each incident follows a state machine (open, acknowledged, investigating, resolved or false positive). The operator outcomes become labels. CD4ML's "model as a separate service" pattern lets Streamlit, Grafana and the alerting component share one model ([CD4ML](https://martinfowler.com/articles/cd4ml.html)).

Sensor-health rules (stale feeds, flatlines, out-of-range values, unit jumps) must run before the anomaly model and send their results to a separate maintenance channel, or dead meters will appear as leaks. ML Test Score warns that source-system changes can silently change what a feature means ([Breck et al.](https://storage.googleapis.com/gweb-research2023-media/pubtools/4156.pdf)). Drift should be monitored through the anomaly-score distribution and alert rate per DMA, using Evidently (Apache-2.0) ([Evidently](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/evidentlyai/evidently@main/README.md)). NannyML's label-free performance estimation targets classifiers and does not fit an unsupervised forest ([NannyML docs](https://github.com/NannyML/nannyml/blob/v0.6.0/docs/how_it_works/performance_estimation.rst)). Alert delivery needs an alert budget. Since 1 October 2026, Meta charges for every WhatsApp utility message, including those sent inside the 24-hour window ([Twilio notice](https://support.twilio.com/hc/en-us/articles/53100480177819-Notice-Changes-to-WhatsApp-s-Pricing-October-2026)), so the tool should send digests and escalations, with email and SMS as fallbacks.

## Read-only, aggregate-only, MoU-first: the route into an NWC pilot

The Data Protection Act 2020 has applied since **1 December 2023**. Its obligations include 72-hour breach notification to the Information Commissioner and to affected people, a mandatory data protection officer for public authorities, annual DPIAs, and restrictions on cross-border transfers. Corporate fines reach **4% of worldwide turnover** ([Chambers 2026](https://chambers.com/downloads/gpg/1129/013_jamaica.pdf)). Enforcement lags. Registration has been paused since December 2025, and the Commissioner told Parliament that full enforcement will not come before 2028 ([Gleaner, Jul 2026](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20260730/data-defence-delay)). NWC will still apply the law in its own legal review. Anonymised data falls outside the Act ([Chambers 2026](https://chambers.com/downloads/gpg/1129/013_jamaica.pdf)). Starting with **DMA, bulk-meter and SCADA aggregates only**, and no customer meter reads, keeps the pilot largely outside the DPA. If customer data is added later, NWC is the controller and AquaWatch a processor under a written contract. Meter IDs must then be pseudonymised, and AquaWatch must notify NWC of any breach quickly enough for NWC to meet its 72-hour deadline. Alerts that drive action against individual customers would also engage the right not to be subject to solely automated decisions. These points need confirmation from Jamaican counsel.

Security reviewers will judge AquaWatch on one question: does it create any path into NWC's operational technology? Water incidents have exploited exposed OT and weak passwords. At Aliquippa in 2023, IRGC-linked actors compromised an internet-facing Unitronics PLC, and CISA's advice was to change default passwords, require MFA and disconnect PLCs from the internet ([CISA AA23-335A](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa23-335a)). At Oldsmar in 2021, a TeamViewer intrusion was reported, though it was later disputed ([Cisco](https://blogs.cisco.com/security/oldsmars-cyber-attack-raises-the-alarm-for-the-water-industry)). In 2024 an IT breach at American Water paused billing ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/american-water-confirms-hack-customer-portal-and-billing-services-suspended/)). The accepted pattern is **one-way, read-only export** from a historian replica in the OT DMZ. There is no inbound connection, VPN or write-back, and AquaWatch has no setpoint or command capability. The design should be documented against IEC 62443 zones and conduits ([ISA](https://www.isa.org/standards-and-publications/isa-standards/isa-iec-62443-series-of-standards)) and NIST SP 800-82r3 ([NIST](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/82/r3/final)), with controls mapped to NIST CSF 2.0 and a completed AWWA self-assessment ([AWWA](https://www.awwa.org/resource/cybersecurity-guidance/)). Jamaica has no cybersecurity act yet, but JaCIRT has finalised a national standards framework and is seeking powers to require standards and incident reporting ([Observer, Oct 2026](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2026/10/21/jamaica-braces-cyber-stress-test/)). The Cybercrimes Act also criminalises access beyond what is authorised ([Chambers 2026](https://chambers.com/downloads/gpg/1129/013_jamaica.pdf)), so the authorised scope must be written down.

The commercial path matters as much as the technical one. Paid work with NWC falls under the Public Procurement Act 2015 and GOJEP ([Ministry of Finance](https://www.mof.gov.jm/?p=4879)). An unpaid university research collaboration under an MoU likely does not, though this needs legal confirmation. The pilot package should contain:

- an MoU covering one or two DMAs for three to six months, with a named NWC champion, IP and publication terms, and the Access to Information Act position
- a data-sharing agreement listing exact fields, hosting region and deletion at pilot end
- a mutual NDA, because network topology is itself sensitive
- a security annex
- success and exit criteria agreed up front

The pilot should then run in three phases: an offline back-test on a historical extract, shadow-mode alerts that staff review but do not act on, and live alerts with field verification. Regional funders are the natural sponsors. **71% of IDB Lab Source of Innovation projects** have a utility partner from the design stage ([IDB](https://www.iadb.org/en/topics/water-and-sanitation/initiatives/source-innovation)), and the IDB has already piloted a data-sharing platform for Caribbean utilities with CWWA and CAWASA support ([IDB news](https://www.iadb.org/en/news/idb-pilots-innovative-data-sharing-platform-caribbean-water-utilities)). Every alert should read "possible leak or unexplained consumption, unverified", never "leak detected". Results should be framed in NIST AI RMF terms ([NIST](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework)), since Jamaica has no AI law.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. "Is Isolation Forest accurate?" matters less than "can AquaWatch prove, at a crew-affordable alarm rate, that it finds a leak sooner than an MNF rule would?" Six injected events cannot answer that, a point-wise F1 cannot express it, and training that peeks at labels makes it unanswerable. The work that most improves credibility is therefore mostly evaluation and data engineering, not a better model. A plain MNF-plus-CUSUM baseline scored honestly on BattLeDIM and WNTR-simulated lock-off scenarios would give NWC engineers more confidence than a deep learning model. AquaWatch is also not really competing with TaKaDu or Miya. Its likely role is a transparent, low-cost, Jamaica-aware screening layer for the islandwide performance-based contractors arriving in 2027/28 and NWC's monitoring unit, with open metrics that vendors do not publish.

Two uncertainties should shape the team's next conversations more than any code. The first is what telemetry NWC actually has outside KSA and Portmore. If it is only production meters and monthly billing, the product shifts from leak alerts towards water-balance and apparent-loss analytics (estimated bills, zero-consumption meters, suspected illegal connections). The second is how much of Jamaica's loss is theft rather than leakage, which no public source splits. Asking NWC's NRW unit for one historical DMA extract and its repair log is the highest-value next step. It tests both assumptions and starts the relationship a pilot depends on.
