# Jamaica Non-Revenue Water (NRW) and Water-Loss Context (for AquaWatch Jamaica)

Research date: 2026-11-21. Labels used: **[JA]** = Jamaica-specific fact from a source; **[GEN]** = general industry practice, not specific to NWC. Many Jamaican news URLs are paywalled or summarised by search tools, so figures below were checked against more than one source where possible and conflicts are flagged.

## 1. NWC NRW levels (current and historical), measurement/reporting, and targets

### Takeaway
NWC's islandwide NRW is officially about 70-75% of water production (late 2026). That is among the worst in the region and has not improved in years. Only the two Miya co-managed zones, Kingston & St Andrew (KSA) and Portmore, have been brought down to roughly 36-40%. The official Water Compact 2030 target (April 2026) is at most 65% by 2030 and at most 30% by 2040. Older NWC statements give 30% within an 11-year islandwide programme.

### Cited Findings
- [JA] **Current islandwide level:** NWC officials told Parliament's Public Accounts Committee in October 2026 that NRW averages 70-73% islandwide. Search summary of the [Jamaica Observer, Oct 2026](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2026/10/21/nwc-seeks-51-hike/) and the [Gleaner, 22 Oct 2026](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20261022/water-hike-faces-fire-businesses-tourism-sector-and-opposition-challenge).
- [JA] NWC's Acting VP Operations, Dr Phillipa Campbell-Francis, put current NRW at **70%** at the OUR webinar on Jamaica's water sector on 30 Sept 2026 — [JIS, "NWC Working to Reduce Water Loss" (Oct 2026)](https://jis.gov.jm/nwc-working-to-reduce-water-loss/)
- [JA] NWC's five-pillar revenue reform plan, released alongside its 51% tariff request, states that about 70% of water production is non-revenue — [Jamaica Observer, 25 Oct 2026](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2026/10/25/facing-heat-rate-request-nwc-unveils-5-pillar-plan/)
- [JA] **Government baseline and targets (official):** The Water Compact 2030 says "Reducing NWC's non-revenue water (NRW) levels from its current 75% of water production to at most 65% by 2030 ... By 2040, the target is to reduce NRW to at most 30%." The Ministry of Water, Environment and Climate Change published it on 8 April 2026 — [World Bank Water Forward: Jamaica Water Compact 2026 (PDF)](https://www.worldbank.org/content/dam/theworldbankgroupasset/document/2026/water-forward-compacts/Water%20Compact%20Jamaica_2026.pdf)
- [JA] The same Compact calls the NRW level "unacceptably high". It commits to "performance-based contracts that leverage private sector expertise and link funds to actual gains on the ground", plus capital-intensive mains replacement and knowledge transfer to NWC staff — [Water Compact 2026](https://www.worldbank.org/content/dam/theworldbankgroupasset/document/2026/water-forward-compacts/Water%20Compact%20Jamaica_2026.pdf)
- [JA] The OUR's 2023-2024 annual report put NWC NRW at **74.3%**, with no improvement over the previous six years. This is a search-result summary of Observer reporting and should be verified in the OUR annual report — [Observer via search](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2026/10/21/nwc-seeks-51-hike/)
- [JA] In January 2024, NWC's pre-qualification document for the Islandwide NRW Reduction Programme cited islandwide NRW of **80%**, with a target of 30% — [Gleaner, 12 Jan 2024](https://past.jamaica-gleaner.com/article/business/20240112/water-utility-targets-resorts-and-schemes-decade-long-revenue-improvement)
- [JA] **Conflicting low estimate:** A Gleaner editorial (12 June 2026) on the Auditor General's review says estimated NRW is "40% to 60%". This contradicts the official 70-75% and is probably loose wording — [Gleaner editorial, 12 Jun 2026](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/index%2Ephp/article/commentary/20260612/editorial-nwc-audit-reveals-lie)
- [JA] **System scale:** NWC supplies about 75% of the population with water and about 30% with sewerage. It runs more than 1,000 treatment and pumping facilities and about 11,000 km of pipes, and produces about 190 million (imperial) gallons per day — [Gleaner editorial, 12 Jun 2026](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/index%2Ephp/article/commentary/20260612/editorial-nwc-audit-reveals-lie)
- [JA] The 2019 Water Policy splits Jamaica into Utility Service Areas (piped supply; about 85% of the population) and Non-Utility Service Areas (about 15%) — [Water Compact 2026](https://www.worldbank.org/content/dam/theworldbankgroupasset/document/2026/water-forward-compacts/Water%20Compact%20Jamaica_2026.pdf)
- [JA] **Regional history:** KSA NRW was above 60% (more than 120,000 m3/day lost) at baseline. By January 2023 it was about 40% (under 70,000 m3/day) — [JIS, 30 Jan 2023](https://jis.gov.jm/features/nwc-miya-non-revenue-water-reduction-programme-yielding-positive-outcomes/). A 2019 Gleaner report gave KSA falling from about 60% (2016) to 36% (2019) — [Gleaner, 7 Sep 2019](https://web5.jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20190907/reduction-non-revenue-water-losses-kingston-st-andrew). The two sources disagree on the latest KSA figure (36% vs about 40%).
- [JA] KSA and Portmore co-management brought NRW down to **38-40%**, per NWC in Sept/Oct 2026 — [JIS, Oct 2026](https://jis.gov.jm/nwc-working-to-reduce-water-loss/)
- [JA] **How it is measured:** NWC defines NRW as water "produced, treated and pumped but never reaches a paying customer" (leaks, theft, metering inaccuracy). NRW is reported as a percentage of production / system input volume — [JIS, Oct 2026](https://jis.gov.jm/nwc-working-to-reduce-water-loss/); [JSE Green Bond page, Apr 2026](https://greenbond.jamstockex.com/?p=14044). The co-management programmes also report volumetric losses in m3/day — [JIS, Jan 2023](https://jis.gov.jm/features/nwc-miya-non-revenue-water-reduction-programme-yielding-positive-outcomes/)
- [JA] The Compact's monitoring framework lists "NRW levels" as an indicator. It requires biannual progress reports to the Integrated Water Resource Management Council (IWRMC, set up by Cabinet in July 2020) and plans data integration across NWC, WRA, NEPA, Rural Water Supply Ltd (RWSL) and others — [Water Compact 2026](https://www.worldbank.org/content/dam/theworldbankgroupasset/document/2026/water-forward-compacts/Water%20Compact%20Jamaica_2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- The official figures (74.3% in 2023/24, 70-75% in 2026, 80% in the 2024 tender) mean islandwide NRW has not improved. The gains in KSA and Portmore have been offset by the rest of the island, which suggests NRW outside those two zones is above 75%.
- NRW is reported mainly as a percentage of system input. IWA advises against using percentage NRW as a performance indicator ([GEN], see section 4). This gives a tool an opening to offer volumetric and per-connection KPIs alongside the percentage.

### Gaps
- No NWC annual report with an IWA-format water balance (split into real and apparent losses) was found online. The nwcjamaica.com NRW page could not be fetched because of a TLS certificate error.
- No published islandwide NRW volume in m3/day was found. An approximate figure follows from about 190 MIGD of production at about 70% NRW, but it is not a published figure.
- The OUR annual report 74.3% figure comes from a search-tool summary and was not read in the primary PDF.

## 2. The NWC-Miya NRW co-management contracts (KSA 2015-c.2020; Portmore 2021-2026)

### Takeaway
In July 2015, NWC signed a US$42.5M, five-year performance-based "co-management" contract with Miya, an Israeli firm owned by Arison Investments, to cut NRW in Kingston & St Andrew. Payment was tied to meeting targets. Methods were DMAs and pressure zones, hydraulic-model-based pressure management, active leak detection with NWC crews using Miya equipment, and meter replacement. KSA losses fell from above 60% to about 36-40%, and NWC said this saved about US$8M/year. A follow-on Portmore contract (US$13M, 2021-2026) built 12 pressure zones and 30 DMAs and cut the leak backlog from 3,000 to 150. No independent end-of-contract evaluation of KSA was found.

### Cited Findings
- [JA] **KSA contract:** Signed July 2015; five years; about US$42.5M; with Miya, a wholly owned subsidiary of Arison Investments. Miya is paid only if the NRW reduction targets are met — [PR Newswire (Miya), 2015](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/miya-signs-a-water-efficiency-project-estimated-at-425m-in-jamaica-300111164.html); [Gleaner, 19 Jul 2015](https://past.jamaica-gleaner.com/article/business/20150719/nwc-inks-deal-curtail-non-revenue-water-losses); [JIS, Jan 2023](https://jis.gov.jm/features/nwc-miya-non-revenue-water-reduction-programme-yielding-positive-outcomes/)
- [JA] **KSA targets:** The 2015 baseline was about 54%. Targets were about 37% in two years, 30% in three years and 20% by the end of the project. A 2017 re-baseline put NRW at 59%, with a revised target of 45% in two years and 30% at contract end — [search summary of Gleaner/Observer 2015-2017](https://past.jamaica-gleaner.com/article/business/20150719/nwc-inks-deal-curtail-non-revenue-water-losses); [Observer 2017 (NWC staff NRW training)](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2017/02/14/nwc-staff-to-undergo-non-revenue-water-management-training/)
- [JA] **Funding:** WaterWorld reported that the KSA project was funded through the Inter-American Development Bank (IDB) — [WaterWorld](https://waterworld.com/drinking-water-treatment/article/16199967/jamaica-to-maximize-efficiency-of-water-system-in-ksa-under-425m-contract). The IDB later publicised the programme's WEX "Innovation for Infrastructure" Global Award — [IDB "Every drop counts"](https://www.iadb.org/en/every-drop-counts-jamaicas-non-revenue-water-program-cops-global-award) (page returned 403; title and snippet only).
- [JA] **KSA results:** By 2018, NRW was down by up to 50% in some district metering zones — [Gleaner via PressReader, 15 Mar 2018](https://www.pressreader.com/jamaica/jamaica-gleaner/20180315/282462824450032). By June 2020, NWC was saving about US$8M (J$1.16B) per year — [Gleaner via PressReader, 3 Jun 2020](https://www.pressreader.com/jamaica/jamaica-gleaner/20200603/281505048440807). By January 2023, losses were under 70,000 m3/day (about 40%), down from more than 120,000 m3/day (more than 60%) — [JIS, Jan 2023](https://jis.gov.jm/features/nwc-miya-non-revenue-water-reduction-programme-yielding-positive-outcomes/)
- [JA] **Portmore contract:** Five years; US$13M; NWC and Miya working together since 2021; completion target 2026. The baseline was about 26,000 m3/day lost (about 60%), with a target of 10,000 m3/day (about 30%). System demand fell from about 42,000 to about 32,000 m3/day for the same population and revenue. **12 pressure zones and 30 DMAs** were established; pressure is managed with hydraulic-model software; NWC field teams detect leaks with Miya's equipment and guidance. The leak backlog fell from 3,000 to 150. Of 20,000 planned meters, 4,000 were installed by January 2023. Illegal-connection interventions were planned in Newlands, Christian Pen, Naggo Head, Gregory Park, Christian Gardens and Dyke Road — [JIS, 30 Jan 2023](https://jis.gov.jm/features/nwc-miya-non-revenue-water-reduction-programme-yielding-positive-outcomes/)
- [JA] The NWC NRW web page describes Portmore 2021-2026 as covering a hydraulic model, leak detection and repair, pressure management with DMA establishment, and meter replacement. It gives an average system input of about 40,000 m3/day, of which about 24,000 m3/day (60%) is NRW. This is a search-tool snippet of [nwcjamaica.com/nrw.php](https://www.nwcjamaica.com/nrw.php)
- [JA] Portmore NRW fell from about 26,000 to 19,000 m3/day, about 1.5 million gallons/day saved, by Sept 2022 — [Observer, 1 Sep 2022](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2022/09/01/1-5-million-gallons-of-water-being-saved-per-day-in-portmore). By May 2024, Portmore losses were reported down from 70% to 40%, with work moving to commercial losses through metering, regularisation and disconnections. Over 200,000 gallons/day were still being lost in parts of Newlands — [Observer, 30 May 2024](https://jamaicaobserver.com/2024/05/30/nwc-reduces-losses-portmore); [Gleaner, 24 May 2024](https://past.jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20240524/hellshire-newlands-get-water-supply-infrastructural-improvements-nwc)
- [JA] Miya's country manager, Alvaro Ramalho, cited Portmore's flat terrain and pressure problems as obstacles to stable supply — [JIS, Jan 2023](https://jis.gov.jm/features/nwc-miya-non-revenue-water-reduction-programme-yielding-positive-outcomes/)
- [JA] Jamaica's model has been cited as a template for the Bahamas (Water & Sewerage Corp., New Providence PBC) — [JIS](https://jis.gov.jm/the-bahamas-to-replicate-jamaicas-non-revenue-water-reduction-programme/); [IDB case study, New Providence PBC](https://webimages.iadb.org/publications/english/document/Case-Study-Performance-based-Contract-for-NRW-Reduction-and-Control-New-Providence-Bahamas.pdf)
- [JA] NWC presented KPIs from the KSA and Portmore programmes at the Caribbean Regional Water Loss Conference 2023 (Jermaine Jackson). The deck exists, but the server would not serve it — [CRWLC 2023 deck](https://www.mpu.gov.tt/crwlc2023/files/talks/KPIs%20in%20KSA_Portmore%20NRW%20Co-management%20Programs%20-%20Jamaica%20-%20Jermaine%20Jackson.pdf)

### Inferences
- The KSA contract nominally ran 2015 to about 2020. No source describes a formal close-out, and later reporting through 2023 still calls KSA an active co-management programme, so it was probably extended or rolled into a sustainability phase. This is unconfirmed.
- The KSA end-target of 20-30% was not reached; the best reported level is 36-40%.
- The Miya operating model is DMA + pressure management + an NWC crew doing active leak detection. AquaWatch should fit that workflow: flag DMAs with rising night flow, then send a crew.

- The CRWLC 2023 KPI deck (ILI, l/conn/day and so on) could not be retrieved.

## 3. NWC data and telemetry (DMAs, SCADA, AMI, bulk meters, loggers), tenders and financing (to 2026)

### Takeaway
Public evidence of NWC telemetry is thin. DMAs, pressure zones, a hydraulic model and Miya-supplied leak-detection and instrumentation exist in KSA and Portmore. NWC has been rolling out solid-state customer meters "with capacity for remote reading" since at least 2023. No public source describes an islandwide SCADA, AMI head-end, or logger resolution. The main pipeline is the **Islandwide NRW Reduction Programme**: 11 years, two geographic lots, co-management PBC, with about US$303M of a US$470M plan aimed at NRW. It was "in procurement" as of Oct 2026, with audit and initial works expected from 2027/28. The Water Compact also names "digitalization" and "strengthening data systems" as priorities.

The first phase is a condition assessment of each major water system, which sets targets and sequencing. A joint NWC-contractor co-management team will run the work, with a dedicated NWC monitoring unit. **Performance measures** are the number of customers with continuous supply and the NRW level per system and overall. - Outside KSA and Portmore, NWC probably has limited DMA metering and limited pressure telemetry. Production and bulk-meter data plus monthly billing may be the main data available. ## 4. IWA water balance, ILI, MNF, Active Leakage Control: how a utility uses them and which KPIs a tool should report

### Takeaway
[GEN] The IWA/AWWA M36 water balance splits System Input Volume into authorised consumption and water losses. Water losses are split into apparent losses (theft, meter under-registration, data errors) and real losses (leaks and bursts on mains and services, reservoir overflows). NRW is unbilled authorised consumption plus all losses. Real losses are benchmarked with the ILI (CARL/UARL). They are located operationally through DMA Minimum Night Flow (MNF) analysis and Active Leakage Control (ALC): flag rising night flow, step-test or acoustically survey the DMA, repair, verify that MNF drops. This is the method the Miya programmes used in KSA and Portmore.

### Cited Findings
- [GEN] The IWA water balance and NRW definitions (system input, billed/unbilled authorised consumption, apparent vs real losses) — [World Bank/PPIAF "Basics in NRW: Water Balance" deck](https://ppp.worldbank.org/public-private-partnership/sites/ppp.worldbank.org/files/2022-03/1_Basics_in_NRW___Water_Balance.pdf)
- [GEN] **ILI** = CARL / UARL. **UARL (litres/day) = (18 x Lm + 0.8 x Nc + 25 x Lp) x P**, where Lm is mains length (km), Nc the number of service connections, Lp the private pipe length (km) and P the average pressure (m) — [Autodesk Info360 ILI docs](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/ENU/INF360I-UserGuide/files/GUID-9EAF65E8-2C26-45AC-863B-9A160EA21EEA.htm); [MDPI review](https://mdpi.com/2673-4591/69/1/211)
- [GEN] **ILI bands** (World Bank Institute, developing countries): below 4 means further reduction may be uneconomic; 4 to below 8 is lower priority; 8 to below 16 is medium priority; 16 or above is high priority — [World Bank NRW deck](https://ppp.worldbank.org/public-private-partnership/sites/ppp.worldbank.org/files/2022-03/1_Basics_in_NRW___Water_Balance.pdf). Best achieved values are below about 1.5 — [EU JRC good practice](https://greenbestpractice.jrc.ec.europa.eu/print/pdf/node/587)
- [GEN] **MNF:** Flow is usually lowest between about 02:00 and 04:00. Night leakage = MNF - legitimate night consumption, with night consumption often assumed to be under about 10% of MNF or estimated from smart-meter data — [EU JRC](https://greenbestpractice.jrc.ec.europa.eu/print/pdf/node/587); [PMC paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10758779)
- [GEN] Limits of ILI and percentage NRW, and the case for using several indicators — [TaKaDu, "Let us talk about TE and ILI" (2023)](https://www.takadu.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Let-us-talk-about-TE-and-ILI_Final-16.8.23.pdf); [EU good practices on leakage management](https://circabc.europa.eu/d/a/workspace/SpacesStore/ec13ae01-7800-4114-8d72-98827b509e18/Good%20Practices%20on%20Leakage%20Management%20-%20Case%20Study%20document._Final.pdf)
- [JA] NWC and Miya applied this method in Portmore: DMAs and pressure zones, hydraulic-model pressure control, leak detection and repair (backlog from 3,000 to 150), and metering. They reported losses in m3/day and % — [JIS, Jan 2023](https://jis.gov.jm/features/nwc-miya-non-revenue-water-reduction-programme-yielding-positive-outcomes/)

### Inferences
- [GEN] A tool relevant to NWC should report, per DMA and per day:
  - MNF (m3/h) and its trend and anomaly score
  - Estimated night leakage
  - Real losses in litres per connection per day (and per km of main per day where connection density is under 20/km)
  - NRW in m3/day and as % of system input (NWC's headline metric)
  - ILI where pressure and asset data exist
  - Supply hours per day (continuity, which is also the Islandwide PBC's own KPI)
  - Average zone pressure
  - Leak-detection backlog and repair time
  - Volume recovered after repairs (the "verify MNF drop" loop)
- Including apparent-loss proxies would match OUR's breach data and Portmore's commercial-loss focus: estimated-bill counts, meters with zero or flat consumption, and suspected illegal connections.

### Gaps
- No ILI or litres/connection/day figure for KSA or Portmore was found in public sources. The CRWLC 2023 deck probably has them but could not be retrieved.
- IWA and AWWA M36 primary documents were not fetched; formulas come from secondary sources that agree on the standard coefficients.

## 5. Causes of water loss in Jamaica and how intermittent supply affects data-driven leak detection

### Takeaway
[JA] The drivers named in Jamaica are ageing and broken pipes, widespread theft and illegal connections (including truckers drawing from hydrants and valve tampering), free or "social" water, metering inaccuracy and estimated billing, and frequent scheduled and unscheduled lock-offs (drought rationing, power outages, hurricanes such as Melissa in Oct 2025). Hilly terrain creates high-pressure zones, while flat Portmore has its own pressure problems. [GEN] Intermittent supply breaks the core assumptions of MNF analysis: there is no steady night baseline, pipes drain and refill, tanks fill at night, and meters register air. A Jamaican tool has to be supply-schedule-aware.

### Cited Findings
- [JA] Causes named include leaks from old and broken pipes, theft by consumers and free "social" water — [Gleaner editorial, Jun 2026](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/index%2Ephp/article/commentary/20260612/editorial-nwc-audit-reveals-lie). - [JA] **Illegal connections:** In 2023, NWC removed more than 86 illegal connections (half-inch to one-inch lines), with over 3 million gallons lost on some days. Power outages, high turbidity and outages after Melissa took systems offline — [Gleaner, 26 Oct 2025](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/index%2Ephp/article/news/20251026/nwc-most-major-systems-still-operating-turbidity-and-outages-pose-challenges); [Gleaner, 12 Jan 2026 (Roaring River pump power)](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20260112/work-progress-restore-electricity-nwc-pump-roaring-river)

- [JA] **Terrain and pressure:** Portmore's flat terrain and pressure issues make stable supply hard — [JIS, Jan 2023](https://jis.gov.jm/features/nwc-miya-non-revenue-water-reduction-programme-yielding-positive-outcomes/)

### Inferences
- [GEN] Under intermittent supply:
  - MNF is undefined when the zone is shut at night. When supply resumes, the surge reflects pipe refill and household tank filling, not leakage.
  - Air in pipes makes customer meters over-register.
  - Repeated pressure cycling increases bursts, so outages themselves create leaks.
- A detector should therefore ingest a supply-on/off schedule or infer it from flow. It should mask refill transients, compare like-for-like "supply-on" windows (for example minimum flow during the last hours of a supply window), and treat pump trips and power cuts as known events rather than anomalies.
- Hurricane and drought periods need explicit regime flags, as the OUR's force-majeure suspension shows, so the model is not retrained on abnormal data.
- Sudden sustained flow increases in a DMA can come from illegal connections as well as leaks. A tool should label these "unexplained consumption" rather than "leak".

### Gaps
- No published share of NWC customers on intermittent supply, and no average supply-hours figure, was found.
- No published split of NWC NRW into real vs apparent losses was found, so the relative weight of theft vs leakage islandwide is unquantified.

## 6. Regulators and stakeholders, and OUR performance standards on NRW

### Takeaway
[JA] The **Office of Utilities Regulation (OUR)** is NWC's economic and service-quality regulator. It sets tariffs, including the 20% **K-factor** surcharge for capital works. Its determinations have included NRW reduction targets with a revenue-penalty incentive mechanism (2016 mid-term review). It enforces Guaranteed and Overall Standards, which cover meter reading, meters and outage notice, but there is no customer-facing NRW standard. Other stakeholders include the **Ministry of Water, Environment and Climate Change (MWECC)**, the **Water Resources Authority (WRA)**, NEPA, the Ministry of Health, RWSL, NIC, IWRMC, the Development Bank of Jamaica (PPPs), the Auditor General, and Parliament's Public Accounts Committee. NWC's 51% five-year tariff application (Oct 2026) is now before the OUR.

### Cited Findings
- [JA] The Compact's regulator roles:
  - **OUR:** economic and service regulator, being strengthened in tariff methodology and financial oversight.
  - **NEPA:** wastewater effluent.
  - **WRA:** abstraction regulator, set up under the Water Resources Act 1995; prepared the 2022 National Water Resources Master Plan.
  - **Ministry of Health:** drinking-water quality.

  MWECC (Water Policy and Monitoring Branch) coordinates the Compact. The IWRMC was set up by Cabinet in July 2020 — [Water Compact 2026](https://www.worldbank.org/content/dam/theworldbankgroupasset/document/2026/water-forward-compacts/Water%20Compact%20Jamaica_2026.pdf)
- [JA] **OUR NRW incentive mechanism:** In January 2017 the OUR said NWC was falling short on NRW reduction. Its determination modified the incentive mechanism so that missed NRW reduction further reduces NWC's allowed revenue — [Gleaner, 13 Jan 2017 (search summary; direct fetch timed out)](https://past.jamaica-gleaner.com/article/business/20170113/our-non-revenue-water-flowing-wrong-direction); [OUR NWC Mid-Term Determination Notice, 5 Dec 2016](https://our.org.jm/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/national_water_commission_mid-term_determination_notice_dec_5_2016.pdf) (has a section on "Revenue and Non-Revenue Water Losses")
- [JA] The OUR publishes NWC "Performance Targets & Tariff Requirements". NWC's 2019-2021 tariff submission includes an appendix on performance against OUR standards and targets — [OUR document page](https://our.org.jm/document/national-water-commissions-performance-targets-tariff-requirements/); [NWC tariff submission 2019-2021](https://our.org.jm/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/nwc_tariff_submission_2019-2021_1.pdf); [OUR 2018-2021 interim tariff determination (Dec 2019)](https://our.org.jm/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/nwc_2018_-2021_interim_tariff_determination_final_-_20191210_-1-1-1.pdf); latest annual price adjustment: [ANPAM Determination 2025](https://our.org.jm/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/NWC-ANPAM-Determination-Notice-2025-Final-signed.pdf)
- [JA] **Service standards (Q1 2026):**
  - **Guaranteed Standards:** compensation is four times the service charge or a set amount. They cover meter reading (no more than 2 estimated bills), complaints (30 working days), meter repair (20 working days), new connection (10 working days) and meter installation (30 working days).
  - **Performance Targets:** 12 hours' notice for planned outages of up to 4 hours (98% compliance), and 24 hours' notice for outages over 4 hours (90%). NWC achieved 50% on the latter in Q1 2026.

  Source: [OUR Q1 2026 report](https://our.org.jm/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-Jan-Mar-Quarterly-Performance-Report_FINAL.pdf)
- [JA] **K-factor:** a 20% charge on bills for capital projects — [Gleaner editorial, Jun 2026](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/index%2Ephp/article/commentary/20260612/editorial-nwc-audit-reveals-lie). It is named as a Compact funding source — [Water Compact 2026](https://www.worldbank.org/content/dam/theworldbankgroupasset/document/2026/water-forward-compacts/Water%20Compact%20Jamaica_2026.pdf)
- [JA] **2026 tariff application:** The OUR confirmed receiving NWC's application for a 51% increase over five years. The PSOJ, tourism interests and the Opposition pushed back and demanded better service — [Gleaner, 21 Oct 2026](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20261021/thirsty-cash); [Gleaner, 22 Oct 2026](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/article/news/20261022/water-hike-faces-fire-businesses-tourism-sector-and-opposition-challenge); [Observer, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2026/10/22/psoj-calls-improved-service-pushes-back-nwc-increase-proposal/)
- [JA] **Political oversight:** Matthew Samuda has been the minister responsible for water through 2025-2026 ([Observer, 14 Nov 2025](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2025/11/14/samuda-defends-nwc-figures/)). NWC Chairman Michael Shaw ([Observer, 25 Oct 2026](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2026/10/25/facing-heat-rate-request-nwc-unveils-5-pillar-plan/)). Opposition spokesman Hayles has criticised NWC management ([Observer, 25 Jun 2026](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2026/06/25/hayles-questions-govt-approach-water-access-nwc-management/)). Auditor General Pamela Monroe Ellis reviewed NWC capital projects ([Gleaner, Jun 2026](https://jamaica-gleaner.com/index%2Ephp/article/commentary/20260612/editorial-nwc-audit-reveals-lie)).

### Inferences
- The 51% tariff case is being heard while the public is asking why 70% of water goes unbilled. NRW evidence and dashboards are therefore politically salient for NWC and the OUR in 2026-2027.
- An NRW KPI pack that can be audited and matches what the Compact and OUR track (NRW % and volume, continuity of supply, estimated bills) would match what both regulator and utility must report.

### Gaps
- The current numeric NRW target in OUR's most recent full tariff determination, and how the NRW incentive or penalty is computed today, were not verified. The 2016 and 2019 determinations exist but their NRW sections were not read.
- No details of NRW targets in the pending 2026 tariff application were found.
